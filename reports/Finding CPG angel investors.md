# Mine Round Announcements to Find Sooz's Beverage Angels

The best information on specific individual beverage angels comes from three places: 2025–2026 round announcements, CPG podcasts and newsletters, and the rosters of a handful of angel collectives that invest only in CPG. Investor databases such as Crunchbase, CB Insights and PitchBook are mainly useful for confirming that a name you already have is still writing checks. The clearest sign that someone is writing pre-seed beverage checks today is being **named, with a brand affiliation, in recent beverage pre-seed or seed announcements, ideally more than once in two years**. Carter Comstock and Andrew Abraham fit that pattern: both were named in a 2025 seed "party round" and co-invested again in May 2026. For Sooz, the Texas beverage money sits in **Austin and Dallas**: Clayton Christopher, Justin Mares, Rogers Healy and the Central Texas Angel Network. Houston's angel groups are generalists, and **TiE Houston Angels is the only one that explicitly lists food & beverage and CPG**. Rice's channels are better as warm introductions and co-investors than as lead beverage money; they include the Rice Angel Network, the new One Giant Leap Ventures fund and the Rice Business Plan Competition judge pool. Public sources turned up **no Houston-based individual with a documented beverage angel portfolio**, so Sooz's own LinkedIn connections and Rice alumni tools are the main way to find local angels. On reaching them, practitioner data puts warm-introduction reply rates at roughly **10–34%, against 2–5% for typical cold email**. The tighter limit is securities law. Under Rule 506(b), the exemption most pre-seed rounds use, a first message to a stranger should build a relationship without discussing round terms. Query's design, in which a person reviews every message and sends it from Gmail, already fits those rules. The gains will come from three changes: sourcing angels from dated evidence, routing intro requests through the founders of brands an angel has backed, and logging when each relationship becomes "substantive."

## Trade press finds beverage angels; databases only confirm them

### Free lists find names, and person pages confirm recent activity

No database covers small consumer and CPG angels well, because the large ones record completed, announced deals and get sparse below Series A. PitchBook claims 12.7M+ company profiles, but a comparison site says its coverage **"thins out sharply below Series A"** ([The GTM Directory](https://thegtmdirectory.com/compare/pitchbook-vs-harmonic)), and a basic seat costs an estimated **$12–15K a year** ([VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/pitchbook-alternatives)). Crunchbase Pro costs about **$49 a month billed annually** ($99 month-to-month) and caps exports at 2,000 rows a month ([SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/crunchbase-pro-review-2026)). Critics, including a competitor, say Crunchbase "tracks what already happened" and returns worse results for small investors than a manual search ([AngelBacked](https://www.angelbacked.co/crunchbase-alternative)). Harmonic (about $500 a month) and Dealroom are built for investors looking for companies, not founders looking for angels ([VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/pitchbook-alternatives); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/dealroom-vs-harmonicai)).

The paid databases earn their keep with person-level pages. CB Insights' page for Carter Comstock, the Freshly co-founder and Olipop investor, shows a seed entry dated **August 7, 2025**. It also shows a **$10M Jesse and Ben's Series A on May 6, 2026**, in which he co-invested with Orgain CEO Andrew Abraham ([CB Insights: Comstock](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/carter-comstock); [CB Insights: Abraham](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/andrew-abraham)). Crunchbase shows his personal investments in a table ([Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/carter-comstock)), and Tracxn has a similar page ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/people/carter-comstock/__9c5WcqE2s3y5WpNJsxQZjO_d3uJGEOry-ahYR4Owzns)).

PitchBook records angel participation even in very small beverage rounds. Dallas-based Sensori's latest deal lists an individual angel next to Johnson & Johnson and Texas's Impact Ventures, although the angel's name is paywalled ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/906668-47)). PitchBook and CB Insights both let users filter investors to "Angel (individual)," and university libraries such as Penn's give students access ([UPenn Lippincott Library](https://faq.library.upenn.edu/business/faq/45788)). The research did not check whether Rice's libraries do the same. Asking a Rice librarian costs one email; the alternative is a license costing five figures.

For finding names in the first place, free tools beat paid ones. NFX Signal publishes community-built lists by sector, stage and city. These include a Food & Beverage seed list of **1,578 investors**, a separate F&B pre-seed list, and an Austin F&B seed list ([Signal F&B Seed](https://signal.nfx.com/investor-lists/top-food-and-beverage-seed-investors); [Signal F&B Pre-Seed](https://signal.nfx.com/investor-lists/top-food-and-beverage-pre-seed-investors)). The Austin list surfaces Justin Mares, Brian Spaly, both Springdale partners, and Texas Venture Alliance president CS Freeland, whose usual check is about $10K ([NFX Austin F&B Seed](https://signal.nfx.com/investor-lists/top-food-and-beverage-seed-austin-investors)). The lists mix VCs with angels; Khosla Ventures GP Samir Kaul, for example, sits on the F&B seed list. Separating the angels out takes manual work.

OpenVC is free to browse and allows about one outreach a day through the platform. Premium reportedly costs $99 a month or $299 a year. Its "Angel investors USA" and "Checks under $100k" lists are useful, though reviewers complain that entries in the long tail are stale ([Angels Partners](https://angelspartners.com/reviews/openvc/); [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/investor-lists/checks-under-100k)).

Check aggregator pages against the primary announcement. PremierAlts lists Quench Ventures as a Diamond Brew investor, yet Quench is absent from the June 2026 round announcement ([PremierAlts](https://www.premieralts.com/companies/diamond-brew/investor-list); [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/06/11/premium-soluble-coffee-startup-diamond-brew-scores-seven-figure-round/)). Niche directories such as CPGInvestorList.com, which describes itself as covering 1,000+ consumer investors, and AngelBacked, which has a Texas food-and-beverage page, rest on vendor claims. Treat them as leads only ([hunted.space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/cpginvestorlist-com); [AngelBacked](https://www.angelbacked.co/market/food-and-beverage/texas)).

LinkedIn Sales Navigator and Apollo find people and contact details but have no portfolio data. Sales Navigator Core costs **$119.99 a month** month-to-month after a February 2026 price increase (about $89.99 a month billed annually), with a 30-day free trial ([Costbench](https://costbench.com/changelog/linkedin-sales-navigator-price-increase-2026-02/)). ### Form D shows who raised, not who invested

SEC Form D is not a source of investor names. Its "related persons" are only the issuer's executive officers, directors and promoters ([SEC: Form D definitions](https://www.sec.gov/info/smallbus/formddefinitions)). One search summary the researchers saw claimed otherwise, and it was wrong.

The form is still useful because it reports offering size, amount sold and the **number** of investors. A peer brand's filing therefore shows whether its round is still open and roughly how big the average check is; 25 investors on a $750K raise, for example, implies about $30K each. EDGAR full-text search covers filings since 2001 and can filter by form type D ([SEC EDGAR FTS FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/efts-faq.html)). That lets Sooz list Texas beverage companies that raised in the past 24 months and look up their listed directors, who at seed-stage CPG companies are often the lead angel or an operator-advisor.

Searching for "a series of Roll Up Vehicles" finds AngelList's single-company investment vehicles ([AngelList Help](https://help.angelliststack.com/raise/ruv/post-close-ruv-management)). Their own filings can point to a syndicate organizer. None of these EDGAR searches was tested live.

## CPG collectives and podcasts concentrate the active check-writers

### The Angel Group and a few peers publish who their people are

The most active and visible source of individual beverage angels is The Angel Group, a food-and-beverage angel community founded by Adam Spriggs and Chris Robb. It grew from about **85 members in 2023** ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/video/taste-radio-studio-interview-with-adam-spriggs)) to about **275 members with 35 investments**, mostly in brands under $1M in revenue, and it reviews about 1,000 opportunities a year ([Taste Radio Ep. 803](https://www.tasteradio.com/episodes/they-backed-poppi-now-the-angel-group-wants-to-back-you)). It charges no carry and no management fees ([The Angel Group](https://www.wearetheangelgroup.com/our-community)). Its venture arm, Supernatural Ventures, was founded in 2025 and has since led Fave's $1M seed and Austin-based Frozen One's $2M seed ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2026/exclusive-fave-lands-1m-seed-round-debuts-at-500-sprouts-stores); [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/30205-frozen-one-raises-2-million-in-seed-funding)).

Several members are named in public sources. Spencer Slaine, a founding member based in Miami, invested in Poppi's July 2021 round. Sources disagree on his typical check: NFX says $5K–$50K, Round Funded says $250K ([NFX Signal](https://signal.nfx.com/investors/spencer-slaine); [Round Funded](https://www.roundfunded.com/en/investor/spencer-slaine)). Jaime (or Jamie) Borteck held C-suite roles at Grillo's, Justin's and Food Should Taste Good, and judged the Expo West 2026 Pitch Slam ([New Hope](https://www.newhope.com/retailers/8-brands-compete-for-prizes-recognition-expo-west-pitch-slam)). Caplight lists two further members, Eleanor Hayden and Sophie Taitt, though no other source confirms them ([Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/investor/wearetheangelgroup)).

The Angel Group's criteria are a real hurdle. It says it wants in-market brands **"outperforming velocity expectations"** with a path to $50M+ in revenue ([The Angel Group](https://www.wearetheangelgroup.com/)). New Hope's Natural List podcast also describes a shift toward $10M+ brands ([New Hope](https://www.newhope.com/investors-service-providers/the-natural-list-investing-in-cpg-a-capital-efficient-good-growth-era)). Sooz probably needs retail velocity data before this group is realistic. The group's "Quick Bites" newsletter makes its activity easy to track meanwhile ([Substack](https://theangelgroup.substack.com/p/quick-bites-november)).

A few other collectives are led by people who write early CPG checks themselves. Rachel Mansfield, an angel since 2018, co-founded GRT SHT Ventures in 2022 with Jordan Carpenter; its holdings include Partake, Omsom and Grüns ([SuperScout](https://superscout.co/investor/grt-sht-ventures)). Greer Tessler and Luke Goldstein run Simple Food Ventures, whose Fund I portfolio includes Cure Hydration, Miracle Seltzer and Aura Bora ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/21686-simple-food-ventures-closes-6-million)). Dace Graham's Atlanta family firm, 1731 MGMT, backed Happypop in March 2025 and Chamberlain Coffee in June 2023, with estimated checks of $100K–$500K ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/investor/895968-46); [SuperScout](https://superscout.co/investor/1731-mgmt)). Jaxon Stuart of Spacestation Investments writes $50K–$1M checks, preferring about $300K, and co-led Painterland Sisters' August 2025 seed ([NFX](https://signal.nfx.com/investors/jaxon-stuart); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/painterland-sisters-closes-seed-funding-round-with-notable-investors-302538427.html)).

Graham, Mansfield and Tessler were the three seed-stage panelists at BevNET Live's June 2024 "Meet the Angels" session. BevNET noted there that **small investors are hard to find because they rely on personal networks** ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer24/meet-the-angels-investment-panel-highlights-beverage-school-june-12-during-bevnet-live-summer-2024/)). AngelList adds little. The only explicitly CPG syndicate found, the Consumer Packaged Growth Syndicate (Jeff Jacobs and Marc Nathan), projects about 20 deals a year but showed zero LPs when crawled ([AngelList](https://venture.angellist.com/consumerpackagedgrowth/syndicate)).

## Texas beverage money is in Austin and Dallas; Rice and Houston provide the introductions

## Strong connectors beat cold email, and cold email must stay under 100 words

Practitioner and vendor data, none of it peer-reviewed, consistently put warm introductions ahead of cold email. Vault Catalyst reports **10–34%** reply rates for warm intros, and **60–80%** when the intro comes from one of the investor's own portfolio founders. Well-targeted cold email gets 2–5% ([Vault Catalyst](https://www.vaultcatalyst.com/blog/warm-intros-vs-cold-emails-vc-data)). VC Boom adds an important qualification. Intros from strong sources, such as an active angel or a portfolio founder, reply at 15–25%. Intros from weak sources, such as a LinkedIn connection or a conference acquaintance, reply at only 5–10%. Excellent pre-seed cold emails, at 8–15%, now match those weak intros ([VC Boom](https://www.vcboom.com/guides/cold-email-vs-warm-intro-data)).

Trust matters most to angels. Angel Capital Association guidance notes that some angels will not consider a deal without a reference from a trusted associate ([ACA](https://www.angelcapitalassociation.org/data/Documents/Resources/AngelCapitalEducation/ACEF_BEST_PRACTICES_Screening.pdf?rev=C7C4)). Yet in ACA's older 2017 survey, **58% of angels cited direct contact with entrepreneurs** as a source of deals ([ACA American Angel](https://www.angelcapitalassociation.org/data/Documents/TAAReport11-30-17.pdf)). A secondhand summary of DocSend data adds that 23% of seed founders raised from at least one investor they first reached cold ([thevcwire](https://thevcwire.com/vc-cold-outreach-works-templates-frameworks/)). The practical takeaway is to rank connectors by how strong the relationship is, not how many there are, and to keep cold email as a careful second channel.

Query's intro-request draft should follow two widely used norms. The first is the double opt-in: the connector asks the angel before making the introduction ([Dave Haynes](https://medium.com/@davehaynes/the-art-and-science-of-email-introductions-111892c2eef3)). The second is a forwardable email of about 100 words that the connector can pass on unchanged. It should say what Sooz does, give one line of proof, explain why this investor, and close clearly with a link, and its subject line should also work as-is ([Seedforge](https://seedforge.com/blog/how-to-get-warm-intros-to-investors); [Evalyze](https://www.evalyze.ai/blog/warm-intro-playbook)).

LinkedIn's official data export contains **1st-degree connections only**, with name, company, position and connection date, plus email where the connection allows it. It can take up to 24 hours to arrive ([ConnectSafely](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/export-linkedin-contacts-connections-guide-2026)). Because it misses second-degree paths, two other routes matter. One is approaching the founders of brands an angel has backed, peer to peer; these are the strongest intro sources, at 60–80%. The other is Rice's alumni tools.

For cold email, the consensus is to be brief. In one vendor's set of 1,847 cold investor emails, those that got replies averaged **87 words**, while ignored ones averaged 183 ([VC Boom](https://www.vcboom.com/guides/cold-email-length-reply-data)). YC's Michael Seibel says an investor should be able to read the email in 60 seconds and that it should come from a company address. He also warns against rapid-fire follow-ups and against asking for an in-person meeting immediately ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors-michael-seibel/)). Founder guides add the rest: a specific subject line under about 60 characters, a first sentence that shows research (such as a named portfolio brand), one key metric, one yes/no ask, no valuation, and never emailing several partners at the same firm ([OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc); [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-cold-email-potential-investors/)).

Sources disagree on how far apart follow-ups should be. They agree that each should be a reply in the original thread carrying real news, because "just following up" gives no reason to answer ([StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-write-the-perfect-email-to-investors)). Query's two follow-ups give three touches in total, which falls within the range these guides recommend.

The single proof line should match how Sooz sells. A January 2026 investor panel said DTC brands are judged on retention and repeat purchases, and retail brands on **velocity against category benchmarks** ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/26/what-investors-want-from-emerging-food-and-beverage-brands-in-2026/)). One guide sets the minimum repeat rate at **30–40% within 90 days** and calls 50%+ compelling ([Food Scientist for Hire](https://foodscientistforhire.com/seed-round-ready-what-food-drink-investors-actually-look-for-before-writing-a-check-in-2026/)). Gross-margin benchmarks range from 30% to 65% depending on the source, against a median of about 52% for public beverage companies ([EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/beverage-financial-benchmark)). With benchmarks that inconsistent, Sooz should state its own numbers and how they were measured, for example "top 10 stores averaged X units per store per week over 8 weeks," rather than claim to beat a blog's benchmark.

All LinkedIn activity has to be manual. LinkedIn's User Agreement and help center prohibit scrapers, bots and browser extensions that automate activity, explicitly including connection requests and messages ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). The length limit for connection notes is disputed (200 vs 300 characters) ([Derrick](https://derrick-app.com/b2b-marketing/linkedin-connection-request-message)). Weekly invitation caps are unpublished, with estimates of about 100–200 ([Waalaxy](https://www.waalaxy.com/blog/linkedin-platform-knowledge/linkedin-invitation-limit)). Query should therefore draft notes of 200 characters or fewer and keep each founder well under 100 invites a week.

## Rule 506(b), not Gmail, sets the outreach ceiling

Most pre-seed SAFE rounds rely on Rule 506(b), which prohibits general solicitation. The SEC says a company can avoid general solicitation by limiting the offering to people with whom it has a **pre-existing, substantive relationship**. Reaching many people without such a relationship makes general solicitation more likely ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/general-solicitation)).

"Substantive" means the company has enough information to evaluate the investor's finances and sophistication, and actually evaluates them. Self-certification alone is not enough ([PSBP Law](https://psbplaw.com/blog/substantive-preexisting-relationships-in-rule-506b-offerings-what-you-need-to-know/)). There is no minimum waiting period, and social-media followers or newsletter sign-ups do not by themselves create a relationship ([Trenam](https://www.trenam.com/general-solicitation-rule-506b-citizen-vc-part-1/)). Practitioners treat a mass email to a scraped or purchased list as general solicitation. A genuinely one-to-one email that makes no offer is a grey area, and counsel should define where the line falls ([McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/How-to-avoid-general-solicitation-in-a-Rule-506b-p)).

This points to a **two-stage design**. In Stage A, the first touch shares facts and traction and asks for advice or a call, with no round terms. In Stage B, the SAFE, terms and data room go out only after a call or questionnaire has established both a substantive relationship and accredited status.

There are two sanctioned alternatives. Rule 506(c) allows general solicitation if every purchaser's accredited status is verified. A March 12, 2025 SEC staff letter simplified verification for offerings with **$200K minimum investments for individuals** plus written representations ([King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/sec-staff-no-action-letter-eases-rule-506c-accredited-investor-verification)), but that minimum is unrealistic for most pre-seed angels. Rule 148, adopted in 2020, treats communications at events sponsored by universities, angel groups, incubators or accelerators as not general solicitation, subject to conditions ([Brownstein Hyatt](https://bhfs.com/insights/alerts-articles/2021/what-demo-day-sponsors-and-presenting-companies-should-know-about-rule-148)). That makes HAN, TiE and university events the lower-risk places to discuss the round openly, provided those conditions are met.

The other rules matter less but still apply. CAN-SPAM has no B2B exemption, and it is unresolved whether a founder's securities pitch counts as "commercial" email. Complying anyway costs little: accurate headers, an honest subject line, a postal address, and an opt-out honored for at least 30 days ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). Texas requires a Rule 506 notice filing within **15 calendar days of the first Texas sale**, with a fee capped at **$500** ([Texas SSB](https://ssb.texas.gov/securities-professionals/regulation-securities/filing-requirements-regulation-d-offerings-texas)).

Google Workspace allows 2,000 messages a day, or 1,500 via mail merge ([Google Workspace](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/kb/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace-000007247)). Bulk-sender rules start at about 5,000 messages a day to Gmail addresses. Spam-complaint rates above **0.1%** hurt delivery, and at 0.3% or higher a sender loses mitigation ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)). Founder-sent angel outreach will never come close to these limits. Still, drinksooz.com also carries retail and customer email, so it needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC records and should never run high-volume sequences.

Volume sequencers such as Apollo and GMass are a poor fit for sending to angels. Apollo is still useful for looking up contact details. Lightweight CRMs cover the record-keeping: Streak works inside Gmail, Attio is free for up to three users, and folk costs about $24 per user per month ([Gritt.io](https://www.gritt.io/blog/best-crm-for-fundraising); [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/best-fundraising-crm-tools-for-founders-7-options-for-managing-your-raise)). None of this is legal advice. The choice of exemption and the line between Stage A and Stage B need a securities lawyer's sign-off before the first investor emails go out. (Query itself never sends; it only drafts for a person to review and send.)

## Conclusion

Sooz's edge will come from dated evidence and its own network of connectors, not from a better database. Public sources cover national beverage operator-angels well and Houston angels hardly at all. The research found no Houston individual and no Rice alumnus with a documented beverage angel portfolio, even though such people almost certainly exist. That makes Sooz's own Connections.csv, the Rice alumni directory and the RBPC judge pool its most valuable data. A useful test for each weekly Query run is how many targets have both a dated beverage check and a named connector, rather than how many names it found.

The findings also show that one missing piece holds back three channels: a lead investor. One Giant Leap will not invest without a qualified lead. HAN expects a lead when the raise is over $1M. The Angel Group wants proof of in-market sales velocity, which a lead's money usually pays for. Following the 2025–26 template of many small checks and then a collective-led seed, Sooz needs two kinds of people. One is a credible beverage operator from Austin or Dallas to anchor the round, which in turn unlocks Rice and Houston money. The other is a wide group of Rice and Houston angels with personal ties to the brand, writing $10–50K checks. Securities law and the conversion data point the same way: personal, low-volume outreach that builds a relationship first both converts best and keeps Rule 506(b) intact. Query's human-in-the-loop design is therefore an advantage. What it still lacks is a relationship log that can document that compliance.
