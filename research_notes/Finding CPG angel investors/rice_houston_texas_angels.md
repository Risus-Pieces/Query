# Rice University, Houston and Texas Angel Ecosystem: Channels and Named Individuals for a Pre-Seed Beverage/CPG Brand (Sooz Beverage)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Method: web search only. WebFetch was blocked, so every finding comes from search-result text and snippets. Facts from third-party aggregators (Startup Intros, Tracxn, PitchBook previews, NFX Signal, CB Insights, Crunchbase, Evalyze, Gritt) are marked as such, because they often disagree with each other. A Rice connection is stated only where a source says so. Confidence: **High** = official or primary source and recent. **Medium** = credible secondary source, or an official source that may be dated. **Low** = aggregator-only, old, or ambiguous identity.

## Q1. Rice-specific channels: how they work, how to get in, and their relevance to consumer/beverage

### Takeaway
Rice's own investing channels are tech and deep-tech heavy, but three of them can work for a Rice-affiliated beverage founder:
- **One Giant Leap Ventures**: Rice's new fund, launched October 2026. It writes checks of up to $500K into Rice-affiliated companies, but only after the company has a qualified lead investor.
- **Rice Angel Network / Cannon Ventures**: run by Rice alumni Kyle Fletcher and Lawson Gow.
- **Rice Business Plan Competition (RBPC)**: about 400 judges, many of them accredited angels, and "consumer products" is one of its five tracks.

OwlSpark has closed (final cohort 2025). The Rice Alliance has a new interim leader, JR Reale. I could not verify a Rice tool called "Owls Connect".

### Cited Findings

**Rice Angel Network (RAN) / Cannon Ventures**
- In March 2019, RAN announced that it would be "powered by Cannon Ventures," the investment arm of The Cannon coworking campus in West Houston. RAN is based out of The Cannon. — [InnovationMap](https://houston.innovationmap.com/rice-universitys-angel-network-to-be-powered-by-cannon-ventures-2631759873.html); [The Cannon blog](https://www.thecannon.com/blog/cannon-ventures-and-rice-angel-network-team-up-to-invest-in-houston-based-startups)
- Kyle Fletcher is RAN's managing partner. Lawson Gow founded The Cannon and Cannon Ventures. InnovationMap reports that both are Rice University alumni. — [InnovationMap](https://houston.innovationmap.com/rice-universitys-angel-network-to-be-powered-by-cannon-ventures-2631759873.html)
- Cannon Ventures described itself as two operations: the Cannon Angel Network ("local angel investors who congregate regularly to hear pitches from early-stage companies in need of seed funding") and the Cannon Venture Studio. Its early startup partners (SEATz, Win-Win, Data Gumbo, SeeHerWork) reportedly received $100K–$400K each in seed funding. — [InnovationMap tag page: Rice Angel Network](https://houston.innovationmap.com/tag/rice-angel-network)
- A recent AngelMatch listing says the RAN–Cannon partnership is still in place and describes RAN as having "strong ties to Rice University alumni." — [AngelMatch](https://angelmatch.io/publication/top-10-investors-in-houston)

- I found no current RAN member roster and no stated consumer or CPG focus. Most coverage is from 2018–2019. — same sources

**One Giant Leap Ventures (OGLV), Rice's new university fund (most relevant new Rice channel)**
- Rice announced One Giant Leap Ventures Fund I in early October 2026. It invests up to $500,000 in Rice-affiliated companies: ventures founded by alumni, students or faculty, or built on Rice-licensed IP. — [Rice News](https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/rice-launches-one-giant-leap-ventures-back-promising-startups-across-university-ecosystem); [onegiantleap.vc](https://onegiantleap.vc/)
- The fund is rules-based. A company must first secure a **qualified lead investor** who vouches for it, and then goes to the fund's Investment Advisory Committee. — [Rice News](https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/rice-launches-one-giant-leap-ventures-back-promising-startups-across-university-ecosystem)
- Leadership: **Adrian Trömel**, a Rice graduate and founder who is also interim head of innovation, leads the fund. **Nafisa Istami**, a Rice alumna, is investment manager. Commitments were finalized in August 2026, and the first investment was the Rice spinout Motif Neurotech. Contact: onegiantleap@rice.edu (institutional address). — [Rice News](https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/rice-launches-one-giant-leap-ventures-back-promising-startups-across-university-ecosystem); [Legal Desire, Hogan Lovells release](https://legaldesire.com/hogan-lovells-cadwalader-advises-rice-university-as-it-launches-one-giant-leap-ventures-backing-startups-across-the-university-ecosystem/)
- The fund accepts both LP equity and philanthropic money, so Rice community members can invest alongside it. — [Rice News](https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/rice-launches-one-giant-leap-ventures-back-promising-startups-across-university-ecosystem)

### Inferences
- **Best Rice path for Sooz:** first land a credible lead angel, likely from the Austin CPG investors in Q3, and then bring in One Giant Leap Ventures as a Rice-affiliated co-investor of up to $500K. This only works if a Sooz founder is a Rice alum, student or faculty member. OGLV's "qualified lead" rule makes the lead the gating step.
- RAN/Cannon is the natural warm Rice-alumni angel channel in Houston. Reach Kyle Fletcher or Lawson Gow through The Cannon. There is no evidence they focus on consumer, so present Sooz as a Houston/Rice alumni story.
- **RBPC is open only to graduate-student-led teams.** If Sooz does not qualify, the judge pool (about 400 people, many of them angels, with a consumer track) is still a networking target. Ways in: attend RBPC and Rice Alliance events, or ask the Alliance or JR Reale for introductions to judges who scored the consumer track.
- **"Owls Connect" workaround:** use Ask an Owl and RAEI, and search the Rice Alumni directory manually for alumni whose titles include "angel," "ventures," "beverage" or "CPG." Ask peer F&B alumni such as the Saint Arnold founders and Delaney Berman for introductions to distributors and investors.

### Gaps
- I found no source for a Rice platform called "Owls Connect". It may be internal or newly renamed. Confirm with Rice Alumni Relations.
- RAN's current member list, check sizes and any consumer deals are not public in the search results.
- I could not find RBPC judge rosters by name (2025 or 2026). The judges page has moved on to 2027, and WebFetch was blocked. The Wayback Machine or a direct request to the Rice Alliance could fill this.
- Lilie's current executive director and the rules of its investment fund were not found.
- I could not verify the "OWL Investment Group" or Robert Winter link. The only match was an unverified RocketReach profile of a California-based Robert Winter.

## Q2. Houston channels: how they work, how to apply, and their relevance to consumer/beverage

### Takeaway
Houston's angel groups lean heavily toward tech, energy and life sciences, and only a few list consumer or food and beverage:
- **Houston Angel Network (HAN)**: oldest angel group in Texas, 100+ members. Lists "consumer" as a sector and has a Consumer portfolio filter, but its 2024–2025 deals were medical.
- **TiE Houston Angels**: explicitly lists Food & Beverage and CPG. Typical check about $250K.
- **Golden Seeds Houston**: only for women-led companies.
- **GOOSE Capital**: technology focus, but funded a consumer RBPC grand prize.

I found no Houston family office with a documented 2023–2026 food or beverage deal. "Texas Women Ventures Fund" appears to be a liquidated 2007 Dallas fund, not a Houston channel.

### Inferences
- **TiE Houston Angels** is the Houston group that most explicitly welcomes food and beverage. Its "assigned lead" model means one champion can pull in others. Reach it through the TiE Houston contact page (Somesh Singh and Tejus Mane).
- **HAN** is worth applying to once Sooz has traction and a raise of at least $250K. With a pre-seed raise above $1M, HAN expects a lead first. Present Sooz as "consumer," which HAN lists as a sector.
- Golden Seeds is relevant only if Sooz has women in meaningful executive leadership.
- GOOSE and Texas HALO are poor thesis fits for a beverage brand. Their leaders are still useful connectors.
- Houston's consumer-specific angels are not organized into a dedicated group. In practice, the consumer capital sits in Austin and Dallas (Q3), and Houston networks are better for local credibility and follow-on money.

### Gaps
- I found no named Houston individual with a documented 2023–2026 personal investment in a beverage or CPG brand. InnovationMap funding roundups for 2024–2026 showed no Houston beverage raises.
- HAN's own published application criteria and pre-seed policy are unconfirmed (aggregator-reported only).
- I found no named TiE Houston Angels sector chairs or leadership committee members.
- GOOSE leadership after 2021 is unconfirmed.
- I found no Houston family office with a consumer or beverage thesis.

## Q3. Texas more broadly (Austin CPG hub, Dallas): channels and the investors active in consumer

### Takeaway
Texas's real consumer/CPG investor density is in **Austin**:
- **Clayton Christopher**: Sweet Leaf Tea, Deep Eddy and CAVU founder, now at Astō Consumer Partners; personal angel deals in beverages.
- **Springdale Ventures**: Genevieve Gilbreath and Dan Graham; $40M Fund II for early consumer brands including beverages.
- **SKU**: the CPG accelerator.
- **Naturally Austin**: CPG community whose board members invest.
- **CTAN**: lists food and beverage and invested in Button vodka.
- **Justin Mares**: Kettle & Fire founder and angel.

**Dallas** has the Access Opportunity Fund (Blake Wiley and Grant Christopher, 2021), SKU DFW/DFW CPG, and a Golden Seeds chapter.

### Cited Findings

**Clayton Christopher / Astō Consumer Partners (Austin)**
- Christopher founded Sweet Leaf Tea, which Nestlé acquired in 2011. He grew Deep Eddy Vodka past $100M in revenue before its 2015 sale. He co-founded CAVU in 2015–16 and stepped back from it in 2019. He is now founder and managing partner of Astō Consumer Partners. — [DC Finance speaker bio](https://dc-finance.com/mr.-clayton-christopher); [Nosh 2015](https://www.nosh.com/news/2015/talking-cash-christopher-oza-and-brett-thomas-form-cavu-primozich-leaves-boulder-brands)
- Astō is Austin-based, was founded in 2024 by Clayton Christopher and **Brian Goldberg**, and is a growth-equity fund for food and beverage, beauty, supplements, household and pet brands. ### Inferences
- **For a pre-seed beverage brand, the most realistic Texas lead check is an Austin operator-angel**, such as Clayton Christopher personally (he has personal beverage angel deals) or Justin Mares ($10K–$250K checks). CTAN is a group alternative that accepts pre-seed and SAFEs but expects customer traction.
- Springdale (about $1M checks, seed to Series A) and Astō (growth equity) are probably too large or too late for Sooz's first round. Springdale's partners are still the best "relationship now, invest later" targets.
- SKU's Austin track needs about $100K in revenue. If Sooz is below that, use SKU's showcase events and mentor network for introductions instead.

- Dallas's Access Opportunity Fund is a CPG-specific lead, but its status after 2021 is unknown.
