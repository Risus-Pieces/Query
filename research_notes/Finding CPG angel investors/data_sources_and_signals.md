## 1. Which databases list individual angels with portfolio data, what is free vs paid, and how good is consumer/CPG coverage?

### Takeaway
No single database covers small consumer/CPG angels well. Crunchbase, PitchBook and CB Insights record *completed, announced* deals and thin out below Series A. NFX Signal and OpenVC are the best free starting points for angel names and tags. CB Insights, Tracxn and Crunchbase *person* pages are useful for checking a specific named angel's recent checks. LinkedIn Sales Navigator and Apollo find people but carry no portfolio data. The highest-yield practical stack is: free Signal/OpenVC lists, then a Crunchbase/CB Insights person check, then a LinkedIn profile check. Trade press supplies names that databases miss (see Q2).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a pre-seed beverage brand, a database is a *verification* tool, not a *discovery* tool. Names are best found in trade press and podcasts (Q2, Q5), then checked for recency on Crunchbase or CB Insights person pages.
- Best free starting points: NFX Signal's F&B pre-seed list (filter for angel/solo tags), OpenVC's "Angel investors USA" and "Checks under $100k" lists, and the free Crunchbase account for person-page lookups. Sales Navigator (30-day trial) is worth it for one focused sprint of Texas/Houston boolean searches (Q7).
- Expensive tools (PitchBook, Harmonic, Dealroom) are not cost-justified for a single pre-seed raise unless accessed through a university (Rice/Jones library), an accelerator, or a friendly VC.

## 2. How to extract named angels from round announcements and trade press, with phrasing patterns and search recipes

Pre-seed and seed beverage announcements on BevNET's PR wire, NOSH, PR Newswire and Daily Coffee News often name individual angels, usually as "Other participants included [Name] ([Title, Brand])" or "Seasoned CPG operators X, Y and Z also participated." Many releases hide names behind phrases like "a group of notable industry angels." The best named-angel yield comes from operator-angels (founders and CEOs of exited or scaled brands) listed with their brand affiliation.

## 3. SEC EDGAR Form D: what it reveals and how it can still help

### Takeaway
Form D's "related persons" are the issuer's executive officers, directors and promoters, *not its investors*. The form reveals offering size, amount sold and the *number* of investors, but not their names. It is still useful for (a) finding recently raising beverage companies in Texas and nationally, whose directors and board members are often angels, and (b) spotting SPV/RUV vehicles (e.g., "… a series of Roll Up Vehicles, LP") whose GP or organizer is a syndicate lead.

### Cited Findings
- Form D Item 3 "Related Persons" has only three relationship checkboxes: Executive Officer, Director and Promoter. Directors include "any person performing similar functions" in unincorporated entities. — [SEC: Guide to Definitions of Terms Used in Form D](https://www.sec.gov/info/smallbus/formddefinitions); [Form D Item 3 continuation page (Justia)](https://forms.justia.com/official-federal-forms/securities-and-exchange-commission/form-d-item-3-continuation-page-related-persons-49878.html)
- Under Rule 405, a "promoter" includes someone who takes the initiative in founding the business, or who receives 10%+ of a class of securities or proceeds for services or property in connection with the founding. — [Form 10-SB quoting Rule 405 (older filing)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001341726/000110524105000015/plana10sba.txt); [SEC Form D definitions](https://www.sec.gov/info/smallbus/formddefinitions)
- Other items: Item 4 industry group; Item 6 exemption claimed (e.g., Rule 506(b)/(c)); Item 12 sales compensation recipients (broker CRD numbers); Item 13 total offering amount, amount sold and remaining; Item 14 number of investors already invested and whether any are non-accredited; Item 15 commissions/finder's fees; Item 16 use of proceeds paid to execs/directors/promoters. The filing is due within 15 days of first sale, with no SEC fee. (Item numbers come from secondary sources and an older form copy, so verify against the current form.) — [NH SOS copy of Form D](https://sos.nh.gov/media/3svnbklk/formd.pdf); [Toppan Merrill: SEC Form D](https://www.toppanmerrill.com/glossary/sec-form-d/); [Angel Investors Network Form D checklist](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/regulatory-compliance/sec-form-d-filing-checklist-accredited-investors)
- EDGAR Full-Text Search covers electronic filings since 2001 and supports exact phrases, Boolean operators, wildcards and a filing-type filter (set it to "D"). — [SEC EDGAR FTS FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/efts-faq.html); [EDGAR search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/)
- Form D is structured XML, so keyword search works less well than filtered queries. — [SPVflow: How to search EDGAR for Form D](https://spvflow.com/blog/sec-edgar-search-guide)
- AngelList Roll Up Vehicles (RUVs) are series of a master partnership named "Roll Up Vehicles, LP." Example legal name: "LD Fund I, a series of Roll Up Vehicles, LP." The GP is Fund GP, LLC, the adviser is Platform Advisor, LLC, and the administrator is Belltower Fund Group. RUVs are invite-only through the company's link. — [AngelList Help: Post-close RUV management](https://help.angelliststack.com/raise/ruv/post-close-ruv-management); [AngelList RUV FAQ](https://help.angelliststack.com/raise/for-investors/ruv-faq)
- RUVs reportedly moved to Rollups.com. — [Rollups: RUV](https://www.rollups.com/ruv)
- **Correction to watch for:** one search summary claimed Form D "lists investors in a raise." This contradicts the SEC definitions above (related persons are execs, directors and promoters only). — [SEC Form D definitions](https://www.sec.gov/info/smallbus/formddefinitions)

### Inferences
- **Recipe A (find raising peers, then their angels/board):** in EDGAR FTS, filter Form type D, last 12–24 months, location Texas, and keywords like "beverage" or "drinks." Then search each filer's name in trade press and LinkedIn for "investor/advisor" mentions. Directors listed on a seed-stage CPG Form D are often the lead angel or an operator-advisor.
- **Recipe B (SPV/syndicate leads):** search FTS for `"a series of Roll Up Vehicles"` or for syndicate SPV naming patterns (e.g., "[Brand] SPV, LLC" / "a series of [platform] LLC"). The related persons on an SPV's own Form D are often the syndicate lead or GP entity, which can point to a named individual.
- **Recipe C (signal of a fresh raise):** Item 13 "amount sold" vs "total offering" plus Item 14 investor count shows whether a peer brand's round is still open and roughly how many angels joined (e.g., 25 investors on a $750K raise implies about $30K average checks).

## 6. Strong vs weak signals that a person is an active pre-seed beverage/CPG angel right now

### Takeaway
The strongest signal is being *named in a 2025–2026 pre-seed or seed beverage/CPG announcement*, ideally more than once, with a brand affiliation. Next come operator-angel status (founder or CEO of a scaled or exited CPG brand), syndicate or community membership (e.g., The Angel Group), and repeat co-investing visible on CB Insights or Crunchbase person pages. Pitch-competition judging and podcast appearances are moderate signals. Generic "angel investor" LinkedIn headlines and list-site inclusion are weak.

### Cited Findings
- **Repeat named participation is visible and dated.** Carter Comstock has a seed entry dated Aug 7, 2025 and a May 2026 Jesse and Ben's Series A co-invest with Andrew Abraham. Both were named in a 2025 seed "party round." — [CB Insights: Carter Comstock](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/carter-comstock); [CB Insights: Andrew Abraham](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/andrew-abraham); [BevNET NOSHscape 2025](https://www.bevnet.com/magazine/issue/2025/noshscape-the-latest-food-brand-news-42)
- **Operator-angels are explicitly labeled in releases.** Examples: "Seasoned CPG operators Alex Sourry, Lily Rogath, and Dom Purpura" (Diamond Brew), and the CEOs of Nutrabolt and Bloom in Leisure Hydration. — [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2026/06/11/premium-soluble-coffee-startup-diamond-brew-scores-seven-figure-round/); [BevNET PR: Leisure](https://www.bevnet.com/pr/2026/04/27/leisure-hydration-closes-oversubscribed-series-a-expands-with-target-whole-foods-heb)
- **Syndicate/community deal volume is quantified.** The Angel Group has made 35 investments, mostly in brands under $1M revenue, and sees about 1,000 opportunities/yr. It appears first on Indie CPG's list of investors with 2+ seed deals in 2025. — [Taste Radio Ep. 803](https://www.tasteradio.com/episodes/they-backed-poppi-now-the-angel-group-wants-to-back-you); [Indie CPG](https://indiecpg.beehiiv.com/p/food-bev-seed-2025)
- **Pitch competition judges are a mixed signal.** BevNET's New Beverage Showdown judge panels skew toward corporates, VCs and retailers. June 2024 judges: Lexy Prosszer (Btomorrow Ventures), Janki Gambhir (Coca-Cola), Max Baumann (Basemakers), John Craven (BevNET), Rachel Krupa (The Goods Mart), Charlie New (Whole Foods). Dec 2023 finals included Kristen Bareuther (Asahi) and Ken Sadowsky (Sr. Advisor, Verlinvest). The 2026 (28th) edition took place June 10–11 in NYC, but its judges were not found. — [BevNET Live Summer 2024 judges](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer24/bevnet-live-summer-2024-new-beverage-showdown-semifinalists-judges-announced); [BevNET Live Winter 2023 judges](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivewinter23/bevnet-live-winter-2023-meet-the-new-beverage-showdown-judges); [NOSH: NBS Semifinals NYC 2026](https://www.nosh.com/video/new-beverage-showdown-semifinals-nyc-2026)
- **Accelerators/pitch programs:** Naturally Rising (formerly Pitch Slam) runs regional rounds including Austin (2026 application deadline March 20, 2026), with national judges from Whole Foods, Sprouts, Thrive Market and NIQ. — [BevNET PR: Naturally Rising 2026](https://bevnet.com/pr/2026/01/21/naturally-network-celebrates-five-years-of-naturally-rising-with-the-2026-pitch-competition)
- **List-site inclusion is weak.** Aggregator investor lists can include investors not in the actual round (PremierAlts/Diamond Brew), and directory long-tail data is often stale (OpenVC review). — [PremierAlts](https://www.premieralts.com/companies/diamond-brew/investor-list); [Angels Partners: OpenVC](https://angelspartners.com/reviews/openvc/)
- **Database recency lag.** Crunchbase records completed deals ("tracks what already happened"). — [AngelBacked](https://www.angelbacked.co/crunchbase-alternative)

### Inferences
Proposed signal hierarchy (my synthesis):
1. **Strongest (score 5):** named in at least one beverage/CPG pre-seed or seed announcement dated within the last 12 months. Bonus for 2+ in 24 months, and for co-investing alongside known CPG funds (Supernatural, Tonic, Midnight, Habitat, Simple Food, Melitas).
2. **Strong (4):** founder or CEO of a scaled or exited beverage/CPG brand who appears in any 2024–26 round (operator-angel). Also active members or leads of a CPG syndicate (The Angel Group, Consumer Packaged Growth).
3. **Moderate (3):** CB Insights or Crunchbase person page shows a dated 2025–26 consumer deal. Podcast guest as an investor in 2024–26. Advisor title at a pre-seed brand on LinkedIn.
4. **Moderate-weak (2):** judge or mentor at New Beverage Showdown, Naturally Rising, SKU, or the Rice Business Plan Competition. These are often corporate or VC staff rather than personal-check writers.
5. **Weak (1):** "angel investor" in a LinkedIn headline with no dated deals, or inclusion on generic list sites (Rho, Shizune, AngelMatch).
- Category-fit modifier: favor angels whose named checks are in *beverage* specifically (hydration, functional soda, coffee, non-alc), and Texas-based or H-E-B-connected operators for a Houston brand.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found that validates which signals predict pre-seed check-writing. The hierarchy above is reasoned, not sourced.
- 2026 New Beverage Showdown judges and 2025–26 Naturally Austin judges were not found.
