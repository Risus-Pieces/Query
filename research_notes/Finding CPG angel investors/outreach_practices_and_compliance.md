# Angel Outreach Practices, Automation Limits, and Compliance (for Sooz Beverage, Houston pre-seed)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: how a pre-seed founder should reach and convert individual angels, which parts can be automated, and the legal constraints (US law). **None of this is legal advice.** Items marked "counsel" should go to a securities lawyer before the system goes live. Source-quality notes: most tactical numbers (reply rates, cadence) come from vendor or consultancy blogs and are directional only. Regulatory items come from SEC, FTC, Google, LinkedIn, Texas SSB pages, or from law-firm summaries of them.

## Q1. Warm intros vs cold outreach: evidence, and how to generate warm intros systematically

### Takeaway
No rigorous, peer-reviewed study exists. Every practitioner and vendor source ranks warm intros well ahead of cold email, with rough reply rates of 10–34% (up to 60–80% for a strong portfolio-founder intro) against about 1–5% for typical cold email. The best-written, well-targeted cold email may reach about 8–15% at pre-seed, which is comparable to weak intros. Angels, especially angel-group members, lean hard on trusted referrals. The system should therefore be built as an intro-generation engine (map connections, write a forwardable blurb, run double opt-in intros, ask for referrals), with cold email as a secondary, carefully limited channel.

### Cited Findings
**Response and conversion data (all directional; definitions differ: reply, meeting, or close)**
- Warm-intro reply rates run about 10–34% overall. Strong intros from a fund's own portfolio founder reach 60–80%. Well-targeted, well-written cold email runs about 2–5% (Vault Catalyst, citing Growleads). — [Vault Catalyst](https://www.vaultcatalyst.com/blog/warm-intros-vs-cold-emails-vc-data)
- VC Boom's headline figures are 31% (warm) vs 8% (cold). It splits intros by source: strong sources (active angel, portfolio founder) 15–25%, weak sources (LinkedIn connection, conference acquaintance) 5–10%. It argues that strong pre-seed cold emails at 8–15% now match or beat weak intros. Vendor data with unclear methodology. — [VC Boom: cold vs warm](https://www.vcboom.com/guides/cold-email-vs-warm-intro-data)
- Across 1,847 cold investor emails sent through a fundraising tool, emails that got replies averaged 87 words and ignored ones averaged 183 words. Vendor dataset; methodology not described. — [VC Boom: length data](https://www.vcboom.com/guides/cold-email-length-reply-data)
- Metal (a fundraising platform) claims warm intros convert at 58%+ and perform 10–20x better than cold. This is the high end of the range and comes from a vendor. — [Metal](https://www.metal.so/collections/warm-intros-vs-cold-email-2025-conversion-data-referral-playbook)
- A DocSend 2025 report is cited secondhand as measuring 8–12% intro-to-meeting conversion. The same source reports that in an analysis of 500+ founders, 23% of seed-stage founders raised from at least one investor first reached through cold outreach. — [thevcwire](https://thevcwire.com/vc-cold-outreach-works-templates-frameworks/)
- Other cold estimates: 1–2% reply for well-crafted cold email to VCs ([Founders' Capital](https://founderscapital8.substack.com/p/cold-emails-to-vcs-have-a-95-non)); 1–5% typical, with 10–15% for founders who do it well ([Nire](https://www.nirehq.com/blog/cold-email-investors-uk-founders)); 10–20% for investors who openly accept cold pitches (from a seller of investor lists, so biased) ([22nd Century Frontier](https://www.22ndcenturyfrontier.com/p/the-cold-email-myth-why-300-investors)).
- NFX's Fundraising Manual says "a warm intro is 100x more effective than a cold email." This is older guidance (about 2022). — [NFX Fundraising Manual](https://www.nfx.com/post/nfx-fundraising-manual). NFX's contact page directs investment inquiries to a warm intro or its Signal network. — [NFX Contact](https://www.nfx.com/contact)
- First Round says a direct referral gets a quicker response, but founders shouldn't be afraid to reach out cold. It invested in a company (Iru) after a cold email in 2018. — [First Round](https://www.firstround.com/)

**Angel-specific evidence**
- Angel Capital Association best-practice guidance lists an angel's own contacts ("network of trust") as a standard deal channel. It notes that some angels will not consider deals sent directly by entrepreneurs without a reference from a trusted associate. — [ACA Best Practice Guidance: Screening (PDF)](https://www.angelcapitalassociation.org/data/Documents/Resources/AngelCapitalEducation/ACEF_BEST_PRACTICES_Screening.pdf?rev=C7C4)
- ACA's American Angel report (2017, older data) gives these deal-flow channels, multi-select: direct contact with entrepreneurs 58%, friends/associates 52%, online/crowdfunding platforms 17%. 24% said independent investing outside groups is their main channel. — [ACA "The American Angel" report (2017)](https://www.angelcapitalassociation.org/data/Documents/TAAReport11-30-17.pdf)
- Hustle Fund's Angel Squad content argues that "founder referrals are the only deal source that actually matters" (opinion piece). — [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-founder-referrals-are-the-only-deal-source-that-actually-matters)

**Systematic warm-intro generation**
- Double opt-in intros: the connector asks the investor first, gets a yes, and only then makes the intro. The practice is commonly attributed to Fred Wilson (2009) and is now a widely followed norm. — [Dave Haynes, "The art and science of email introductions"](https://medium.com/@davehaynes/the-art-and-science-of-email-introductions-111892c2eef3)
- A "forwardable email" is a short, self-contained email the connector can forward unchanged. It saves the connector from summarizing the company and "dramatically increases the likelihood" they help. Put the single most impressive metric in the first sentence. — [StartupFundraising.com: investor introductions](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-get-investor-introductions)
- A suggested four-part forwardable blurb (about 100 words): what you do, one line of proof, why this specific investor, and a clear close with a link. — [Seedforge](https://seedforge.com/blog/how-to-get-warm-intros-to-investors). Make the subject line forwardable as-is. — [Evalyze](https://www.evalyze.ai/blog/warm-intro-playbook). A common failure is a blurb that still forces the connector to rewrite it. — [Adamant Ventures](https://adamantventures.substack.com/p/this-common-mistake-will-crush-your)
- Mapping your own network: LinkedIn's "Download your data" (Settings & Privacy → Data privacy) can export only "Connections." The resulting Connections.csv usually has first and last name, current company, position, connection date, and email when the connection allows it. It covers 1st-degree connections only, not 2nd or 3rd. Guides say the export takes minutes to 24 hours. — [ConnectSafely export guide (2026)](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/export-linkedin-contacts-connections-guide-2026); [Icebreaker docs](https://docs.icebreaker.xyz/docs/export-linkedin-data); [Hyperclapper](https://www.hyperclapper.com/blog-posts/download-linkedin-connections-csv-excel)
- Rice networks. I could not find a Rice platform called "Owls Connect." The closest official tools:
  - **Sallyportal.org**, Rice's professional-development and mentoring platform for alumni, students, and parents. — [Rice Alumni FAQs](https://alumni.rice.edu/rice-alumni-faqs); [CASE award entry (2017, older)](https://case.org/awards/circle-excellence/2017/sallyportal-new-and-improved-networking-rice-owls)
  - **Rice Portal** (riceconnect.rice.edu), which includes the Rice Alumni Directory. — [Rice Alumni: Careers & Networking](https://alumni.rice.edu/careers-and-networking)
  - **Ask an Owl** (askanowl.rice.edu), a Q&A service that matches questions to alumni who reply by email. — [Rice Alumni: Careers & Networking](https://alumni.rice.edu/careers-and-networking)
- Rice's Liu Idea Lab (Lilie) and the Rice Alliance are described as offering connections to Rice alumni investors. — [Rice Graduate School blog](https://graduate.rice.edu/news/current-news/my-favorite-rice-alumni-founded-businesses)
- **OwlSpark** (Rice Alliance accelerator): a free 12-week program with no equity taken, run with UH RED Labs at the Ion. It requires at least one Rice-affiliated founder (student, faculty, staff, or recent alum) and draws on the Rice Alliance's investor network. — [OwlSpark](https://alliance.rice.edu/owlspark); [OwlSpark FAQ](https://alliance.rice.edu/owlspark/faq); [Rice News 2025 cohort](https://news.rice.edu/news/2025/owlspark-announces-students-alumni-and-staff-selected-its-13th-cohort)
- **OWL Investment Group** (Rice Business Plan Competition): long-time RBPC investors and supporters, with 60+ judges and individuals who have invested about $3.5M in close to a dozen companies. Members often serve as advisors and board members. — [RBPC: The Owls](https://rbpc.rice.edu/the-owls)

### Inferences
- Treat Rice affiliation as the main warm-intro asset. Sooz should confirm which founder holds the Rice tie (it determines OwlSpark and RBPC eligibility) and work Sallyportal, Ask an Owl, and Lilie/Rice Alliance staff as connectors.
- A practical pipeline: (1) export Connections.csv from each founder and advisor; (2) cross-reference against a target-angel list, such as CPG operators-turned-angels, Houston angel-group members, and Rice alumni in food and beverage; (3) for each target, find the strongest mutual connector; (4) send the connector a forwardable blurb and ask for a double opt-in intro; (5) after each angel meeting or commitment, ask "who else should see this?"
- A weak intro (a LinkedIn acquaintance) may perform no better than an excellent cold email. Rank connectors by relationship strength, not by count.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or primary-source dataset on angel (as opposed to VC) response rates. All figures above are VC-focused or vendor-generated. The primary DocSend 2025 report was not retrieved.
- A commercial site (angelinvestorsnetwork.com) reportedly claims that 78% of 2024 angel deals came via referral. Methodology not found; not verified.
- "Owls Connect" could not be confirmed as a current Rice platform. It may be an internal or new name, so confirm with Rice Alumni (alumni@rice.edu).

## Q2. Cold email to angels: structure, personalization, cadence, timing, and CPG/beverage specifics

### Takeaway
The consensus structure: a specific subject line (company / what / key metric, under about 60 characters); under about 100–150 words; one line of real traction; why this angel specifically; round basics without valuation; one simple yes/no ask. Send from a company-domain address. Follow up as a reply in the same thread, 1–3 times, spaced about 3–7 business days apart, each time with new information, then stop. Sources conflict on the deck: link (often trackable) vs PDF attachment. For a beverage brand at pre-seed, the "one line of traction" should be a retail or DTC proof point: velocity (units/store/week), number of doors and key accounts, repeat rate, and gross margin after trade spend.

### Cited Findings
**Primary investor guidance**
- YC (Michael Seibel): investors should be able to read the email in 60 seconds or less, since a wall of text gets put off and forgotten. Cover the problem, the solution, launch or growth, market size, co-founders, and any contrarian insight. Use plain language, send from a company email address with your name in it, and treat a deck as optional (common format if included). Older guidance (around 2019). — [YC Startup Library: How to cold email investors](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/65-how-to-cold-email-investors); [YC blog (Seibel)](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors-michael-seibel/)
- Seibel also warns against sending multiple follow-ups in quick succession and against asking for an in-person meeting immediately. — [YC blog (Seibel)](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors-michael-seibel/)
- First Round's cold-email formula (written for sales outreach, not fundraising, but transferable): short personalized subject, relevant opener, segue to value, simple CTA, entire email under 100 words. — [First Round on X](https://x.com/firstround/status/1919746652471968140)

**Founder-guide consensus (vendor and practitioner blogs; individual claims could not all be traced to one specific page, so citations are grouped)**
- Subject line: specific beats clever. Use the format "Company / What you do / Key metric," keep it under about 60 characters so it reads in a phone notification, and avoid "investment opportunity." Mention a mutual connection only if it is genuine. — [Capitaly](https://www.capitaly.vc/blog/the-art-of-the-cold-email-to-investors-subject-lines-that-get-opens); [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-cold-email-potential-investors/); [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc)
- Body: lead with one top metric plus one context metric and skip hype words. The first sentence should prove research, such as a specific portfolio company, post, or interview. Keep the whole email under about 150–200 words. — [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-cold-email-potential-investors/); [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-cold-email-investors); [LeadLoft (19 tips from VCs & angels)](https://www.leadloft.com/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors)
- The ask: one next action, ideally a yes/no question or two or three proposed times. Give the raise amount and any commitments or lead, but avoid stating a valuation in a cold email without commitments or a term sheet. — [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc); [Sheetventure](https://sheetventure.com/blog/how-to-write-a-compelling-cold-email-to-vcs)
- Deck handling conflicts. Some guides say never attach (link after sign-off), others say attach a PDF, and others recommend a trackable link such as DocSend. — [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc); [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-cold-email-potential-investors/); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/how-to-cold-email-an-investor-and-actually-get-a-reply/)
- Do not email several partners at the same firm at once; one guide says it produces an instant strike. — [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc)
- If you are very early (only a prototype or waitlist), consider angels and accelerators rather than VCs. — [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc)

**Follow-up cadence (sources conflict)**
- One polite follow-up after 5–7 business days, as a reply in the original thread. — [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-write-the-perfect-email-to-investors)
- Other guides suggest following up after 3 days with a maximum of 2–3 nudges; one follow-up 3–7 days later with a new data point, then stop; or 3–4 follow-ups over 10–12 days, each with a small update. — Grouped from [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-cold-email-potential-investors/), [Capitaly](https://www.capitaly.vc/blog/the-art-of-the-cold-email-to-investors-subject-lines-that-get-opens), [Zyner](https://zyner.io/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors), [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/how-to-cold-email-an-investor-and-actually-get-a-reply/) (exact page-to-claim attribution not verified)
- Consistent principle: each follow-up should carry news (a new customer, retail account, milestone, or hire). "Just following up" gives no reason to reply. — [Visible.vc](https://visible.vc/blog/how-to-cold-email-potential-investors/); [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-write-the-perfect-email-to-investors)
- A general (not investor-specific) sample of 500 outbound campaigns found a 2% response rate for a single cold email, rising to 18% with up to four follow-ups. This was quoted in one of the investor cold-email guides surfaced; I could not verify the original page or study. — e.g., [LeadLoft](https://www.leadloft.com/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors) (attribution unverified)

**CPG/beverage metrics angels want**
- An investor panel (FoodNavigator, Jan 2026) said the growth metric depends on channel: retention and repeat for DTC, velocity and category benchmarks for retail. They also warned that thin margins struggle to scale if the MSRP cannot absorb cost increases. — [FoodNavigator 2026](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/26/what-investors-want-from-emerging-food-and-beverage-brands-in-2026/)
- Velocity (units per store per week) is the number buyers and backers use to judge whether a product works. For early validation, track the top handful of stores even with quarterly POS data. — [CPG Guy: How to track sales](https://www.cpg-guy.com/how-to-track-sales-as-a-cpg-brand). Doors show trial; velocity shows return purchases. — [BrandLab360](https://brandlab360.substack.com/p/the-three-numbers-your-cpg-investor)
- Velocity rules of thumb (unsourced): 1–2 units/store/week mediocre, 3–5 good, 5+ excellent. — [Foundra](https://www.foundra.ai/funding/food-and-beverage)
- Repeat rate: 30–40% within 90 days is the minimum for most early-stage investors, and 50%+ is compelling ([Food Scientist for Hire](https://foodscientistforhire.com/seed-round-ready-what-food-drink-investors-actually-look-for-before-writing-a-check-in-2026/)). Another guide sets the bar at 40%+ within 90 days ([Foundra](https://www.foundra.ai/funding/food-and-beverage)).
- Gross margin conflicts by source: 50%+ after slotting ([Ellty CPG](https://www.ellty.com/blog/cpg-investors)); 40%+ required and 55%+ "clearly fundable" for institutional food VCs ([Ellty Vancouver](https://www.ellty.com/investors/vancouver-food-and-beverage-investors)); at least 30–50% at seed ([ThatRound](https://www.thatround.com/post/raising-your-seed-round-food-and-beverage)); 50%+ for natural grocery and 65%+ for premium DTC ([Food Scientist for Hire](https://foodscientistforhire.com/seed-round-ready-what-food-drink-investors-actually-look-for-before-writing-a-check-in-2026/)); a public beverage-comp median of about 52% ([EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/beverage-financial-benchmark)).
- Margins scale with volume: one estimate gives 20–30% at 500 units/month versus 55–65% at 50,000. — [Foundra](https://www.foundra.ai/funding/food-and-beverage). Show COGS at 10x and 50x volume, and for DTC show CAC, AOV, and retention. — [ThatRound](https://www.thatround.com/post/raising-your-seed-round-food-and-beverage); [Food Scientist for Hire](https://foodscientistforhire.com/seed-round-ready-what-food-drink-investors-actually-look-for-before-writing-a-check-in-2026/)
- Pre-seed investors may accept a strong concept with LOIs, while seed investors want sales data. — [Ellty CPG](https://www.ellty.com/blog/cpg-investors). Small proof points (a pop-up, a pilot, a repeat order) help. — [ThatRound](https://www.thatround.com/post/raising-your-seed-round-food-and-beverage)
- Distributors such as UNFI and KeHE reportedly drop products that don't sell consistently, so distribution depth only counts if velocity holds. — [CPG Guy: PE sale](https://www.cpg-guy.com/starting-a-cpg-brand-for-a-future-private-equity-sale)

### Inferences
- A good Sooz cold or forwardable email template would read: subject "Sooz: [category] beverage, [X] doors / [Y] u/s/w"; line 1 says why this angel (CPG background, Rice/Houston tie, a portfolio brand); line 2 is the product in plain words; line 3 gives one retail or DTC proof point plus a margin or repeat stat; line 4 is a soft ask ("Would you be open to a 20-minute call to see if it's a fit?"). Given the securities-law issue in Q5, the first touch to a stranger should probably not state round terms.
- Use a 3-touch maximum (day 0, about day 5–7, about day 14–21), each later touch carrying a real update such as a new retailer, reorder, or velocity data. Stop after that and move the contact to a quarterly "update" list only if they opted in.
- Benchmarks conflict, so state Sooz's own numbers with their basis (e.g., "top 10 HEB stores averaged X u/s/w over 8 weeks") rather than claiming to hit a blog benchmark.

### Gaps
- No credible data found on best send day or time for investor emails specifically.
- No angel-specific (vs VC) published template from a top-tier source. NFX and First Round have no investor cold-email template in these results; the First Round formula is for sales.
- No source gave pre-seed-specific numeric thresholds for beverage. Most benchmarks are seed or Series A and from advisory blogs.

## Q3. LinkedIn: connection-note best practice and the automation and scraping limits

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's User Agreement (Section 8.2) and help center prohibit third-party bots, scrapers, and browser extensions that scrape data or automate activity, explicitly including sending connection requests and messages. The compliant approach: (a) use LinkedIn's own data export for your 1st-degree connections; (b) research profiles manually or use tools working through LinkedIn's official APIs; (c) write and send every connection request and message by hand. Note length (200 vs 300 characters) and weekly invite caps (about 100–200) are undisclosed or disputed, so design for a low, human-paced volume.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn's help article on prohibited software lists scrapers, bots that act on accounts, and software that circumvents security features or usage limits as violations of the User Agreement (citing Section 8.2). The bot prohibition covers activity such as contacts, messages, comments, likes, shares, and posts. Reusing site content without the owner's consent (including via data brokers) is also prohibited. — [LinkedIn Help: Prohibited software and extensions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)
- LinkedIn does not allow third-party software or browser extensions that scrape, modify the site's appearance, or automate activity. If an account is restricted, the user should disable the tool, and the account is re-enabled at the time stated in the notice. — [LinkedIn Help: Automated activity](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/90586)
- Secondary analysis: Section 8.2 targets specific mechanisms (scraping, circumventing access controls, "bots or other unauthorized automated methods"), not "automation" as a word. Tools using LinkedIn's official API are generally treated as sanctioned. — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/linkedin/linkedin-user-agreement/prohibition-on-scraping-and-automated-data-collection/); [ConnectSafely (2026)](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/is-linkedin-automation-safe-tos-scraping-guide-2026). Third-party vendor views, not legal determinations.
- Connection-note length is disputed. Many guides say 300 characters ([GetFuzzy](https://getfuzzy.ai/blog/linkedin-connection-request-note-300-characters)); another says 200, citing LinkedIn help as checked September 2026 ([Derrick](https://derrick-app.com/b2b-marketing/linkedin-connection-request-message)). Free (Basic) accounts get a limited monthly allowance of personalized notes, and sources and even LinkedIn help reportedly conflict on the number. — [Derrick](https://derrick-app.com/b2b-marketing/linkedin-connection-request-message); [GetFuzzy: message limits](https://getfuzzy.ai/blog/linkedin-message-limits)
- LinkedIn applies weekly invitation limits but doesn't publish them. Third-party estimates run about 100 to 200 per week, and Premium reportedly doesn't raise the cap meaningfully. Restrictions typically last about a week. — [Waalaxy](https://www.waalaxy.com/blog/linkedin-platform-knowledge/linkedin-invitation-limit); [Derrick: premium connection limit](https://derrick-app.com/linkedin/profile/premium-connection-limit)
- LinkedIn's official "Download your data" export of Connections is the sanctioned way to get your own network into a CRM. — [ConnectSafely export guide](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/export-linkedin-contacts-connections-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- The compliant LinkedIn workflow: export Connections.csv per founder or advisor and load it into the CRM. Use automation off-platform only: matching against target lists, enriching from public non-LinkedIn sources, and drafting a 200-character note the founder pastes and sends by hand. Never use extensions or "LinkedIn automation" SaaS that click or send for you, or that scrape profiles or search results.
- Note content for an angel: a specific reason (shared Rice tie, their CPG exit, a mutual connection) plus a light non-offer opener ("Fellow Owl building a Houston beverage brand; would value your perspective on retail launch"). Do not pitch the round in a connection note to a stranger (see Q5).
- Keep to well under about 100 invitations per week per founder, with high acceptance rates, to avoid restrictions.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the current full text of the LinkedIn User Agreement Section 8.2. Wording comes from LinkedIn help articles and secondary sites.
- The final outcome of hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn was not retrieved in this session, so verify it before citing the case.
- No authoritative current figure for note length or the weekly invite cap. Check the counter in the invite dialog.

## Q4. Angel groups: Houston Angel Network, TiE Houston Angels, and the application-to-pitch process

### Takeaway
HAN runs a monthly cadence: apply online at least one month before the target pitch; a sector deal committee (including a Consumer committee) screens in the last week of the month with a 30–60 minute reviewer call; an internal vote on the first Wednesday picks three companies; they pitch on the third Wednesday (no pitches in July or December) for 8 minutes plus 5 minutes of Q&A. If applied by about October 18, 2026, the earliest realistic HAN pitch is November 18, 2026. TiE Houston Angels runs an online application, then member or leadership pre-screen, then a monthly virtual Investor Forum pitch, with 1–2 angels leading diligence. Both groups' members invest individually.

### Cited Findings
**Houston Angel Network (current official page)**
- Eligible companies are raising pre-seed, seed, bridge, or Series A via equity, debt, or debt with equity. To be "seriously considered" they need a working prototype and market validation (pilot, beta users, or revenue), with industry-dependent exceptions. — [HAN Entrepreneurs](https://www.houstonangelnetwork.org/entrepreneurs)
- An application fee applies and does not guarantee a pitch or funding. A free readiness self-assessment tool is offered. HAN itself takes no equity, and each member decides individually. — [HAN Entrepreneurs](https://www.houstonangelnetwork.org/entrepreneurs)
- Process: (1) apply via "Apply for Funding" at least one month before the target pitch date; (2) a sector deal committee (Energy, Life Sciences, Consumer, Tech, Aerospace) meets in the last week of each month and assigns a member reviewer for a 30–60 minute call; (3) the reviewer presents at the internal selection meeting on the first Wednesday of each month, where three companies are selected; (4) pitch meetings on the third Wednesday of each month, except July and December, with 8 minutes of pitch and 5 minutes of Q&A. — [HAN Entrepreneurs](https://www.houstonangelnetwork.org/entrepreneurs)
- An older HAN page (likely outdated) listed a $250 presentation fee if selected; $250K–$1.5M equity raises; exclusions (entertainment, real estate development, bars/restaurants/spas); a request that ventures raising over $1M secure a lead first; and a suggestion to contact the Managing Director with an executive summary before applying. — [Old HAN Weebly page](https://houstonanglenetwork.weebly.com/entrepreneurs.html)
- CB Insights lists HAN's range as $250K–$3M equity and requires a Texas base or a commitment to relocate. These figures conflict with the old page. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/investor/houston-angel-network)
- HAN also runs a biannual summit where about 11 startups pitch. — [InnovationMap](https://houston.innovationmap.com/houston-angel-network-hears-startup-pitches-2645286326.html)
- A generic third-party estimate puts Houston angel rounds at 45–90 days from pitch to close. Not HAN-specific. — [Gritt.io](https://www.gritt.io/blog/angel-network-houston)

**TiE Houston Angels**
- Process: online application; pre-screen by Houston members with industry experience; pitch to the Houston chapter; then due diligence led by 1–2 Houston angels. Criteria: fast-growing markets, proven product-market fit, a relevant team, US registration, and a raise heading toward seed or Series A co-investment. — [TiE Houston Angels](https://houston.tie.org/programs/tie-houston-angels/)
- The entrepreneurs page says the leadership team reviews every opportunity, pitches happen at a monthly virtual Investor Forum (investor attendance by invitation), and a deck, financials, and fundraising details are required before follow-up. — [TiE Houston: For Entrepreneurs](https://houston.tie.org/for-entrepreneurs/). (The two pages describe screening differently; both are over a year old.)
- To reach TiE Angels globally, a startup must first present to its nearest chapter and draw member interest. — [TiE Angels: Apply for Funding](https://angels.tie.org/apply-for-funding/)

## Q5. Legal and compliance: Reg D 506(b) vs 506(c), general solicitation, demo days, CAN-SPAM, Texas

### Takeaway
Most pre-seed SAFE or priced rounds rely on Rule 506(b), which forbids general solicitation. The SEC treats this as a facts-and-circumstances question. The main safe path is to offer only to people with whom the company has a pre-existing, substantive relationship, meaning one formed before the offer and with enough information to actually evaluate the person's finances and sophistication. Mass or automated cold emails to strangers that discuss the offering are high-risk and likely general solicitation. A single personalized relationship-building email without offering terms is a grey area, and counsel should define the line. The alternatives:
- Rule 506(c), which allows general solicitation but requires reasonable steps to verify every purchaser is accredited. A March 2025 SEC staff letter eased this with $200K-per-individual minimums plus representations.
- Pitching at qualifying sponsor events under Rule 148 (angel groups, universities, accelerators).

CAN-SPAM covers "commercial" email (no B2B exemption). Whether a securities pitch is a "commercial product or service" is unresolved, so the safe default is to comply: accurate headers, honest subject, postal address, and an opt-out. Texas requires a Rule 506 notice filing within 15 days of the first Texas sale, with a fee capped at $500.

### Cited Findings
**General solicitation and the relationship standard**
- The SEC's small-business guidance: a company can avoid general solicitation by limiting an offering to investors with whom it (or its broker-dealer or investment adviser) has a pre-existing, substantive relationship. Whether general solicitation occurred depends on facts and circumstances. Reaching many people with whom the company has no relationship increases the likelihood that the outreach is general solicitation. — [SEC: General solicitation (building blocks)](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/general-solicitation); [SEC PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/general-solicitation-building-blocks.pdf)
- "Pre-existing" means formed before the offering began, or established through a broker-dealer or investment adviser before that professional's participation. "Substantive" means the issuer has enough information to evaluate, and does evaluate, the prospective investor's financial circumstances and sophistication. Self-certification alone (checking a box) is not enough. — [PSBP Law](https://psbplaw.com/blog/substantive-preexisting-relationships-in-rule-506b-offerings-what-you-need-to-know/); [Trenam Part 1](https://www.trenam.com/general-solicitation-rule-506b-citizen-vc-part-1/); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/ag-deal-diary/sec-provides-guidance-on-general-solicitation-in-c-dis-and-no)
- A founder-oriented law firm's formulation: a relationship is "preexisting" if formed before discussing your offering, and "substantive" if you know enough to evaluate the investor's accredited status. — [Varnum LLP](https://www.varnumlaw.com/insights/four-ways-to-avoid-general-solicitation-in-private-offerings/)
- SEC staff (2015 C&DIs and Citizen VC letter): there is no minimum waiting period. A waiting period is neither necessary nor sufficient; the quality of the relationship and a substantive vetting process matter. Social-media followers or bare mailing-list sign-ups do not by themselves establish a relationship. — [Trenam Part 1](https://www.trenam.com/general-solicitation-rule-506b-citizen-vc-part-1/); [Trenam Part 2](https://www.trenam.com/general-solicitation-under-rule-506b-after-citizen-vc-part-2/); [Mintz on Citizen VC (Aug 2015, older)](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2015-08-10-citizenvc-no-action-letter-clarity-and-guidance-conducting); [Dechert (2015)](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2015/9/sec-provides-guidance-on-activities-that-constitute-general-sol.html)
- Citizen VC (an August 2015 SEC staff letter) accepted an online process that built relationships before any offer: a public site with no offering content, plus questionnaires and vetting before investors saw deals behind a password. — [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2015-08-10-citizenvc-no-action-letter-clarity-and-guidance-conducting); [D&O Diary guest post (2016)](https://www.dandodiary.com/2016/06/articles/securities-laws/guest-post-general-solicitation-under-rule-506b-after-citizen-vc-clearer-guiding-principles-analyzed-and-future-best-practices/)
- Under the 2015 interpretations, participating in a demo day or venture fair is not automatically general solicitation, and a presentation that makes no offer is not a solicitation. — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/ag-deal-diary/sec-provides-guidance-on-general-solicitation-in-c-dis-and-no)
- Practitioner views on cold outreach: a mass email to a purchased or scraped list is general solicitation. Cold emails to strangers are a classic example to avoid under 506(b). One securities attorney says email blasts to several hundred people are general solicitation even where relationships exist (unverified against SEC text). The SEC's analysis focuses on the relationship rather than how the person was first contacted, so a genuinely one-to-one, non-offer email is a grey area. Factual business information that does not condition the market for an offering is generally not an offer. — [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/How-to-avoid-general-solicitation-in-a-Rule-506b-p); [Syndication Attorneys FAQ](https://syndicationattorneys.com/faqs/what-are-the-rules-regarding-506b-solicitation-and-mass-communication/); [Fundamentals.law](https://www.fundamentals.law/p/506-b-who-can-you-raise-money-from); [Venture South: Perils of General Solicitation](https://www.venturesouth.vc/perils-of-general-soliciation); [Mayer Brown overview (2019)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2019/08/on-point--general-solicitation.pdf) (exact claim-to-page attribution among these is approximate)
- One practitioner guide warns that general solicitation (or adding a 506(c) track) can taint 506(b) reliance for that offering. — [PPM Lawyers: 506(b) vs 506(c) 2026 guide](https://ppmlawyers.com/506b-vs-506c-guide/)

**506(c) alternative**
- Rule 506(c) permits general solicitation if the issuer takes reasonable steps to verify that every purchaser is accredited. Traditional methods include reviewing IRS forms or bank and brokerage statements, or getting written confirmation from specified professionals. — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/sec-staff-no-action-letter-eases-rule-506c-accredited-investor-verification); [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/sec-no-action-letter-streamlines-verification-steps-for-rule-506c-offerings-with-sufficiently-large-minimum-investments)
- SEC staff no-action letter (March 12, 2025, to Latham & Watkins): an issuer may treat "reasonable steps" as satisfied if it requires minimum investments of $200,000 for natural persons ($1M for entities, with variants), obtains written representations of accredited status and that the minimum is not third-party financed, and has no contrary knowledge. — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/sec-staff-no-action-letter-eases-rule-506c-accredited-investor-verification); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/insights/2025/03/SEC%20Issues%20Guidance%20on%20Use%20of%20Minimum%20Investment%20Amounts%20for%20Verifying%20Accredited%20Investor%20Status); [Cahill](https://www.cahill.com/publications/client-alerts/2025-06-11-sec-staff-issues-no-action-letter-addressing-accredited-investor-status-verification-in-rule-506-c-offerings-based-on-minimum-investment-amounts)

**Demo days: Rule 148 (adopted November 2020, Release 33-10884)**
- Under Rule 148, communications at a seminar or meeting sponsored by a college or university, a state or local government, a nonprofit, or an angel investor group, incubator, or accelerator are not deemed general solicitation, subject to conditions:
  - Sponsors may not make investment recommendations, negotiate investments, or take compensation beyond reasonable administrative fees.
  - Online participation is limited to members, accredited investors, or invitees chosen for industry or investment experience.
  - Content about the offering is limited.
  - An "angel investor group" must be accredited investors who hold regular meetings, have defined (possibly written) processes, and are unaffiliated with brokers or advisers.
  — [Brownstein Hyatt (2021)](https://bhfs.com/insights/alerts-articles/2021/what-demo-day-sponsors-and-presenting-companies-should-know-about-rule-148); [Wyrick Robbins](https://www.wyrick.com/news-insights/sec-adopts-amendments-to-expand-the-communications-exemption-for-demo-days-and-general-solicitations-of-interest); [ACA blog](https://angelcapitalassociation.org/blog/the-exempt-offering-ecosystem-what-the-sec-changed)

**CAN-SPAM**
- A "commercial electronic mail message" is one whose primary purpose is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a commercial product or service. — [15 U.S.C. § 7702 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7702)
- Primary-purpose rules for mixed messages (16 CFR 316.3): a message is commercial if a recipient reading the subject line would likely conclude it advertises a commercial product or service, or if the transactional or relationship content does not appear at the beginning of the body. — [16 CFR 316.3 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/316.3); [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-316/section-316.3)
- The FTC guide says CAN-SPAM applies to all commercial messages, including business-to-business email, with no B2B exemption. Requirements: accurate header and "from" information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification as an ad, a valid physical postal address, a clear opt-out that works for at least 30 days after sending, honoring opt-outs, and monitoring third parties who send on your behalf (both are liable). — [FTC: CAN-SPAM Act Compliance Guide for Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- Transactional or relationship messages are largely exempt but still may not contain false or misleading routing information. — [FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business); [16 CFR 316.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/316.3)
- The only securities-adjacent commentary found treats paid stock-promotion emails as advertisements. It is about penny-stock promoters, not founders, so it is a loose analogy. — [Securities Lawyer 101](https://securitieslawyer101.com/2020/can-spam-investor-relations)

**Texas**
- The Texas State Securities Board requires a Rule 506 notice filing (a copy of the federal Form D plus a fee) through NASAA's Electronic Filing Depository within 15 calendar days after the first sale in Texas. The fee is 1/10 of 1% of the aggregate offering, capped at $500. No annual renewal or sales report. Federal Form D amendments must be mirrored. — [Texas SSB: Filing Requirements for Regulation D Offerings](https://ssb.texas.gov/securities-professionals/regulation-securities/filing-requirements-regulation-d-offerings-texas)
- The Texas exemption for Rule 506 offerings sits at 7 TAC §109.13(k) (seen in a 2018 rulemaking page; final text not confirmed). — [Texas SSB Board Rules, June 8, 2018](https://www.ssb.texas.gov/texas-securities-act-and-board-rules/board-rules/june-8-2018)
- 506(b) basics (unlimited accredited investors; up to 35 non-accredited but sophisticated purchasers with disclosure obligations; no general solicitation): see the SEC's 506(b) page. It surfaced in search but its full text was not retrieved here, so verify. — [SEC: Private placements, Rule 506(b)](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/private-placements-rule-506b)

### Inferences
- **Recommended compliance posture (for counsel to confirm):**
  1. Choose the exemption up front. Most likely 506(b), limited to accredited investors, so the non-accredited disclosure burden never applies.
  2. Design outreach as two stages. Stage A is relationship-building without offering terms: who we are, traction, a request for advice or a call. Stage B is the offer, with the SAFE, terms, and data room, only after a call or questionnaire establishes a substantive relationship and accredited status, all logged in the CRM.
  3. Never send automated bulk emails or posts announcing "we're raising $X" to strangers or public audiences.
  4. Keep public channels (website, LinkedIn posts, newsletters) free of offering details.
  5. If Sooz wants broad public outreach (posts, mass email, platforms), switch to 506(c) with verification. A $200K individual minimum is unrealistic for most pre-seed angels, so third-party verification letters are more likely.
  6. Use angel-group pitches and university or accelerator demo days that qualify under Rule 148 for broader exposure.
- Record keeping: the CRM should log, for each angel, how they were sourced, the date of first contact, the date the relationship became substantive (call or questionnaire), accredited status and how it was determined, and when offering materials were first shared. Practitioners note this is what counsel will want to see. — supported by [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/How-to-avoid-general-solicitation-in-a-Rule-506b-p)
- Even if CAN-SPAM's application is unclear, complying costs little: real name and domain, accurate subject, Sooz's postal address in the signature, and a line like "If you'd rather not hear from me, just reply and I won't follow up." Honor it in the CRM as a do-not-contact flag. One-to-one, individually written emails to a known contact are less likely to be viewed as "commercial advertisement," but no source resolves this.
- Also file the federal Form D (generally within 15 days after the first sale under Rule 503) alongside the Texas notice. Rule 503 timing was not retrieved here; confirm with counsel.

## Q6. Tooling: Gmail-native, mail merge, CRMs, sequencers, and deliverability

### Takeaway
At pre-seed angel scale (dozens to low hundreds of targets), a Gmail-native setup is enough and safest: an inbox-based CRM (Streak) or a lightweight relationship CRM (Attio, folk, or Notion/Airtable), with drafts created programmatically and sent by hand. Mail-merge and sequencing tools (YAMM, GMass, Apollo) are built for volume, which is exactly what creates securities-law general-solicitation risk and deliverability risk. Google now enforces SPF, DKIM, and DMARC plus spam-rate thresholds (above 0.1% hurts, at or above 0.3% loses mitigation). Workspace caps are 2,000 messages/day, 1,500 via mail merge. Sooz's main domain (drinksooz.com) carries its retail and customer email, so it must not be burned.

### Cited Findings
**Gmail limits and Google sender rules**
- Google Workspace sending limits: 2,000 messages/day per user, 1,500 for mail merge, 500 for trial accounts. Exceeding them blocks sending for 24 hours (the account stays accessible). — [Google Workspace KB: Gmail sending limits](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/kb/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace-000007247)
- Consumer Gmail is about 500 per rolling 24 hours, and each recipient counts separately (one email to 5 people = 5). — [GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/gmail-sending-quota-still-send-your-campaign/) (vendor)
- Google defines a bulk sender as one sending about 5,000+ messages to personal Gmail accounts in 24 hours, counted across the primary domain including subdomains. The status is permanent once reached. User-reported spam rates above 0.1% hurt inbox delivery, and from June 2024 bulk senders above 0.3% are ineligible for mitigation until they stay below 0.3% for 7 consecutive days. One-click unsubscribe is required only for marketing or promotional messages. — [Google: Email sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)
- Google's requirements include SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication, valid forward and reverse DNS, and one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders. — [Help Net Security (Oct 2023)](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2023/10/04/gmail-bulk-senders-requirements/); [GMass: bulk sender guidelines](https://www.gmass.co/blog/gmail-bulk-sender-guidelines/)
- Third-party deliverability blogs disagree on how hard 0.3% is enforced. One claims outright 550 rejections since November 2025, and another says Google documents no automatic rejection from spam rate alone. — [EmailListValidation](https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/0-3-percent-spam-rate-threshold-google-postmaster/); [Suped](https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/compliance/how-likely-is-gmail-to-enforce-the-less03percent-spam-rate-limit-within-the-next-year)

**Mail merge (YAMM, GMass)**

Sample stages: Identified → Initial Outreach → Intro/Warm Up → ... → Committed / Passed. — [Streak for startup founders](https://www.streak.com/industries/startup-founders); [Gritt.io CRM comparison](https://www.gritt.io/blog/best-crm-for-fundraising)

### Inferences
- **Recommended stack for Sooz:**

Mass sending also raises the general-solicitation problem in Q5. And lookalike domains look odd for a consumer brand raising from local angels. If Sooz ever uses a sequencer, restrict it to single-recipient, manually approved steps that stop automatically on reply.

## Q7. What to automate vs keep human

### Takeaway
Automate the research and bookkeeping layer: list building from public sources, enrichment, connection mapping from LinkedIn exports, draft generation, CRM updates, reminders, reply detection and tagging, and compliance logging. Keep human every act that touches the investor relationship: approving and sending each email, all LinkedIn actions (contractually required), intro requests to connectors, calls and meetings, judging when a relationship is "substantive" and an investor is accredited (legally required), and sending offering documents.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn prohibits bots and automated methods for connection requests, messages, and engagement, and prohibits scraping. LinkedIn-side actions must therefore be manual. — [LinkedIn Help: Prohibited software](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387); [LinkedIn Help: Automated activity](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/90586)
- A "substantive" relationship requires the issuer to actually evaluate the prospect's financial circumstances and sophistication; self-certification alone is not enough. That is a judgment step that cannot be delegated to a bulk process. — [PSBP Law](https://psbplaw.com/blog/substantive-preexisting-relationships-in-rule-506b-offerings-what-you-need-to-know/); [Trenam Part 1](https://www.trenam.com/general-solicitation-rule-506b-citizen-vc-part-1/)
- Mass emails to scraped or purchased lists are viewed by practitioners as general solicitation. — [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/How-to-avoid-general-solicitation-in-a-Rule-506b-p); [Syndication Attorneys FAQ](https://syndicationattorneys.com/faqs/what-are-the-rules-regarding-506b-solicitation-and-mass-communication/)
- Spam complaints above 0.1% hurt deliverability, and at or above 0.3% senders lose mitigation. Templated mass sending raises complaint risk. — [Google sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)
- Some angels won't look at deals without a trusted reference, and intros via relationships far outperform cold outreach. Relationship work is the high-value human activity. — [ACA Best Practice Guidance](https://www.angelcapitalassociation.org/data/Documents/Resources/AngelCapitalEducation/ACEF_BEST_PRACTICES_Screening.pdf?rev=C7C4); [Vault Catalyst](https://www.vaultcatalyst.com/blog/warm-intros-vs-cold-emails-vc-data)
- Investor-side norms: research each investor and reference something specific; don't email multiple partners at one firm; don't send rapid repeated follow-ups. These are judgment calls that automated sequences routinely violate. — [YC (Seibel)](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-cold-email-investors-michael-seibel/); [OpenVC](https://www.openvc.app/blog/how-to-cold-email-vc)
- Double opt-in intros depend on the connector asking the investor first, a human-to-human step. — [Dave Haynes](https://medium.com/@davehaynes/the-art-and-science-of-email-introductions-111892c2eef3)

### Inferences
| Layer | Automate (safe and valuable) | Keep human |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing | Build target lists from public sources (angel-group member pages, Rice/RBPC pages, CPG exits, AngelList-type profiles, press); dedupe; score fit (CPG/beverage experience, Houston/Texas, Rice, check size) | Final decision on who belongs on the list |
| Network mapping | Parse each founder's and advisor's LinkedIn Connections.csv (official export); match to targets; rank likely connectors | Asking a connector for an intro; choosing which connector |
| Enrichment | Look up professional email and company data via compliant APIs (e.g., Apollo enrichment, not LinkedIn scraping); verify emails to keep bounces low | Judging relevance; never scraping LinkedIn |
| Drafting | Generate personalized first drafts and forwardable blurbs from CRM fields plus Sooz's current metrics; enforce length and subject-line rules; strip round terms from first-touch drafts to strangers | Editing for voice and accuracy; final send from the founder's Gmail |
| Cadence | Schedule follow-up reminders (e.g., day 5–7 and day 14–21); stop automatically on reply or opt-out; surface "news" from metrics for follow-ups | Deciding whether a follow-up adds value; sending it |
| Inbox | Detect and classify replies (interested / pass / later / opt-out / intro offered); update CRM stage; draft suggested replies | Responding, scheduling, conversations |
| Compliance log | Timestamp first contact, source, relationship-established date, accredited-status method, when offering docs were shared; flag any record where offer materials were sent before a relationship was logged | Evaluating sophistication and accreditation; counsel's sign-off on process; deciding 506(b) vs 506(c) |
| Investor updates | Assemble monthly update drafts from metrics for opted-in contacts | Approving content; keeping offer terms out of broad updates unless counsel approves |

- Guardrails to build in: a hard daily send cap (e.g., 20–30 founder-sent investor emails), no CC/BCC blasts, a one-email-per-firm rule, automatic suppression of opt-outs, and a "stage gate" so the data-room or SAFE link can only be sent to contacts marked "relationship established + accredited verified." The caps are an inference meant to stay far below Google's limits.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found comparing human-sent vs automated-sequence outcomes for investor outreach specifically.
- Whether AI-drafted but human-sent outreach performs differently from fully hand-written outreach: no credible data found.
